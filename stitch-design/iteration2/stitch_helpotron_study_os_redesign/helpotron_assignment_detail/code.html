Synthesis: The Krebs Cycle

The citric acid cycle, also known as the TCA cycle or Krebs cycle, is a series of chemical reactions to release stored energy through the oxidation of acetyl-CoA derived from carbohydrates, fats, and proteins.

1. Acetyl-CoA Entry

The cycle begins with the transfer of a two-carbon acetyl group from acetyl-CoA to the four-carbon acceptor compound (oxaloacetate) to form a six-carbon compound (citrate).

Acetyl-CoA + Oxaloacetate + H₂O → Citrate + CoA-SH

2. Key Oxidative Steps

  • Isocitrate is oxidized to α-ketoglutarate, producing NADH and releasing CO₂.
  • α-ketoglutarate is oxidized to succinyl-CoA, producing another NADH and CO₂.
  • Succinate is oxidized to fumarate, yielding FADH₂.
